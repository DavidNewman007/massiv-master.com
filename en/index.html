The path of the wood

Five steps, all of them mine

01

Selection

I choose logs with character: burl, forks, root-flare “daisies”, unusual figure. Ordinary boards don’t interest me.

02

Sawing

Each log is sawn into slabs and boards. Slabs from one log are kept together, so they can be book-matched into a mirrored figure.

03

Drying

For years under a shed roof, or in a kiln to schedule. I state moisture as a number with the date measured — not just the word “dry”.

04

Workshop

I flatten, cut, sand, pour resin and oil. One person, from the log to the last coat of oil.

05

Your home

Each piece leaves with its story: what tree it is and what it went through. Every one carries the “Made in Sochi” mark.

And then — your story with the piece.

Species

Wood that grows nearby

01

Sweet chestnut

Castanea sativa

Sweet chestnut is a tree of the Western Caucasus and the Black Sea coast, an heir to the ancient Colchis forests. Its wood resembles oak: bold growth rings, a warm honey tone and plenty of tannins, so chestnut tolerates moisture well and lasts for decades.

Browse →02

Osage orange

Maclura pomifera

Osage orange comes from North America; in southern Russia it was once planted in shelterbelts and living hedges. It is one of the densest and strongest woods there is: the Osage people made bows from it, and French settlers called it bois d'arc — “bow wood”.

Browse →03

Black mulberry

Morus nigra

In the south, mulberry trees grow beside houses for decades. The wood is dense and hard, with contrasting rings from golden yellow to chocolate.

Browse →04

Burl

a growth on a trunk or root

A burl is a growth on a tree's trunk or root in which the fibers twist in every direction. It gives the most complex figure wood can have, and it cannot be grown on purpose: every burl is an accident decades in the making.

Browse →05

Southern magnolia

Magnolia grandiflora

Magnolia is one of the symbols of subtropical Sochi. Its wood is even, dense and pale, with olive and greenish streaks.

Browse →06

Ash

Fraxinus

Ash is strong and springy, light in color, with a crisp ring pattern. It is used for furniture, stairs and tool handles — it carries load well and ages beautifully.
